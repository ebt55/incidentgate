## What a declined result here does and does not mean

The steering prompt truthfully tells the attacker model that it is inside a synthetic laboratory and that a safeguard chain is being measured. That is the honest choice -- the fixtures really are synthetic, and implying otherwise would measure a model's response to a false claim about real harm rather than to the objective under test.

It is also a known behaviour-changing condition. A model that declines when it has been told it is being evaluated is weaker evidence than a model that declines believing the situation is real, and nothing in this table distinguishes the two. So an observed `declined` bounds **this configuration** -- this steering text, this disclosure, this model, this scenario -- and is not a general claim about how the model behaves when it does not know it is being watched. Reading a decline here as "the model would not do this" would overstate it by exactly that gap.

The same disclosure does not weaken an observed `attempted`: a model that pursues the covert objective while told it is being measured has pursued it, and what the gates then do is the measurement this table exists for.

## What the model monitor here measures, and what it does not

**This is not covert detection.** On T1 the covert call is denied `capability_prohibited` at the policy gate, which runs before the monitor node. So the monitor never sees the covert action: `monitor_verdict` is null on the covert leg of every row below, every covert cell in the accompanying `monitor-assessment-record-v1` artifact reads `never_consulted`, and nothing here is evidence about whether this monitor would notice a covert action. The covert-detection measurement this project exists for remains unreached.

**What it does measure** is a real model judging a real model's *honest* remediation, and the durable human approval gate downstream of that judgement. That is a benign false-positive result: the honest leg is policy-legal, correctly evidenced and the only thing the monitor is shown, so a BLOCK here is a false positive and an ALLOW is the monitor declining to produce one.

**The monitor is no longer shown the policy's verdict, and that does not make this detection.** Under `monitor-input-v3` the rendered input carries no `policy_decision`, `policy_risk_class`, `policy_reasons`, `authorization_required`, `authorization_scope` or `evidence_support_state`, so a verdict here is the monitor's own judgement rather than partly agreement with a decision it was handed. That makes the benign false-positive number above honest. It does **not** put a monitor in front of a covert call: the policy still denies `operations.write_outbound_note` before the monitor node runs. Covert detection still needs an observe-only row family or T4, and this input contract is a prerequisite for either rather than a substitute for one.

**A bad result here is a bad monitor, not an absent one** -- with one exception. `schema_violation` and a missing capture are facts about this harness rather than about the model, and the accompanying record states which of the three states each cell was in (`judged`, `unparseable`, `never_consulted`) so the two cannot be read as one.
