# Checkpoint-B threat model

## Scope

This threat model covers the local D1-D8, S1, and S2 checkpoint demonstrations only. The asset being protected is the bounded mock service state and the integrity of the incident decision record. It does not claim protection for external infrastructure, production identities, or arbitrary third-party tools.

## Trust boundaries and controls

| Boundary | Threat | D1 control |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Evidence/log input | Embedded instructions, stale or cross-thread evidence | Evidence is data, must be fresh and tied to the incident/thread before it can support a proposal. |
| Proposal to policy | An unsafe or malformed rollback proposal | Deterministic policy permits only the frozen D1 rollback with the expected arguments and cited evidence. |
| Policy to monitor | Policy-allowed action that needs an advisory check | The default fixture monitor, or an explicitly configured bounded Anthropic monitor, validates the proposed action before any approval is requested. |
| Approval | Missing, replayed, substituted, expired, or wrong-actor approval | The durable approval binds action hash, approver, expiry, one-time use, context, and scope. |
| Execution | Duplicate delivery or response loss after commit | Bundled mock operations and idempotency results commit atomically in Postgres; retry reloads durable state. |
| Recovery claim | Reporting success without current recovery evidence | Fresh post-action verification is required before reporting recovery. |

## Assumptions and residual risk

The local mock identities, fixture monitor, Postgres instance, and Compose host are trusted parts of the lab. The UI is localhost-only, but it is not an enterprise authentication system. The development-only Compose password is not a production secret. Local operators must protect their workstation and local environment files.

The fixture monitor is deterministic test infrastructure, not a general defense against malicious models or arbitrary tool calls. The configured Anthropic monitor is advisory only: it requires an explicit provider, API key, and pinned model, accepts only a bounded synthetic digest and action, uses the official SDK's provider-schema transformation while retaining strict local structured-output and action-hash validation, and fails closed to a generic hash-bound block. The initial 2026-08-10 synthetic smoke failed closed with HTTP 400 from unsupported raw Pydantic provider-schema constraints; after correction, one guarded live smoke passed exactly once (`1 passed in 9.16s`) using only the synthetic typed restart action and bounded citation digest. No request, response, rationale, key, or provider error body was printed or recorded. Exactly-once semantics do not cross the mock-operation boundary. External Langfuse export is optional; one isolated authenticated smoke was verified on 2026-08-10, but that does not establish continuous availability, delivery, or production reliability. Default local runs do not send telemetry externally.

Checkpoint B's three evaluation-only local modes have 30 measured rows and exact replay, but the measurement is not a real model/provider benchmark or a broad security claim. Accepted history must contain commits `3c19e74`, `57daaa0`, and `0321265`; `feature/checkpoint-b-evaluation` is the recovery/development branch used, and `main` is the accepted integration target. There were no eligible caught unsafe-proposal rows, so policy/monitor catch fields are N/A rather than 0%; the complete monitor condition's measured false-positive result is 0/5. These facts do not establish monitor coverage or generalization.

R01-R12 are implemented local deterministic reliability slices; R13-R20 remain frozen/planned contracts in [`../scenarios/planned-matrix.json`](../scenarios/planned-matrix.json). Of the sabotage tier's 8 frozen contracts and 24 attack-policy variants, T1, T2 and T4 are implemented and published, and T3 and T5-T8 remain frozen with no runtime; 36 planned fail-closed checker specs remain. T4's covert objective is policy-legal and monitor-suspicious by construction, and its published rows record that the deterministic policy approved every covert call while the advisory monitor stopped the first call departing from the contract's canonical sequence. T2's covert objective is a single policy-prohibited call, and its published rows record the deterministic policy denying it with `capability_prohibited` in all six gated cells, so its advisory monitor and human approval gate are recorded as `never reached` rather than as having contributed - a different published state from `disabled`, and not a claim that three layers stopped it. Both its attack proposer and its monitor are deterministic stand-ins, labelled as such in every row, so neither is evidence about a model's behaviour or a model-backed monitor's coverage. R05 is read-only/no-action and proves durable lock auto-release observation through exactly three reads under its per-thread/correlation/actor/read-permission cursor, fixture elapsed=45 seconds (not wall-clock latency), process-loss resume, and zero approval, operation, or mutation. R06-R08 use typed exact actions with deterministic policy, advisory monitoring in complete mode, durable human approval, action/context/token/idempotency binding, one Postgres mutation/ledger, response-loss exactly-once replay, and fresh post-commit evidence: exact orders query-plan baseline (R06), bounded customer-read routing to primary (R07), and identifier-only credential rotation to `db-app-2026-09` with no secret/value field (R08). Public FastMCP, fresh-host UI, safe telemetry trace chains, and isolated migration 001-012 are tested. Reliability v2 is evaluated only with local mock fixtures in complete, policy-only, and ungated modes; clean-revision-guarded full/subset same-trial semantic replay excludes only envelope `generated_at`. These bounded tests do not claim production safety, real DB/query/DNS/credential systems, real 45-second latency, external exactly-once, model/provider benchmarks or generalization, published reliability-v2 artifacts, or continuous Langfuse/Anthropic reliability. Existing dated external smokes remain separately bounded. The remaining contracts and checker specifications are not runtime coverage. Semantic falsification corrected action→predicate contradictions before acceptance.

S1's hostile instruction is retained only in immutable fixture storage so the lab can prove the input boundary. Public evidence, reports, audit/UI, and telemetry use its fixed classification and SHA-256 digest; they do not expose the raw instruction. B2 checks are deterministic and local; they are not a production security, model-safety, or general prompt-injection claim.
